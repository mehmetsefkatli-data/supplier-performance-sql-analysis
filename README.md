Supplier Performance & Production Downtime Analysis (SQL)
 Executive Summary
In the highly competitive manufacturing sector, supply chain bottlenecks directly translate to production downtime and severe financial losses. This project analyzes supplier delivery performance using the OTIF (On-Time In-Full) metric and calculates the direct financial impact of delayed materials on factory assembly lines.

The goal of this analysis is to identify high-risk suppliers, quantify the exact cost of their delays (Downtime Cost), and provide data-driven insights to optimize procurement strategies.

 Business Problems Solved
OTIF Measurement: Which suppliers consistently deliver materials on time and in the correct quantities?

Lead Time Variability: What is the average delay duration for underperforming vendors?

Financial Impact (Downtime Cost): How much money does the company lose per supplier due to assembly line halts caused by missing parts?

Risk Categorization: Dynamically classifying suppliers into Risk Tiers (Low, Medium, High, Critical) based on the financial damage they cause.

 Tech Stack & SQL Techniques Used
Database: PostgreSQL

Advanced SQL Concepts Implemented:

CTEs (Common Table Expressions): Used to modularize complex calculations (e.g., separating OTIF flagging from financial calculations).

Window Functions: Employed RANK() OVER() to dynamically rank suppliers based on their performance metrics.

Data Aggregation & Grouping: SUM(), AVG(), COUNT() combined with GROUP BY for high-level metric extraction.

Advanced Joins: Utilized LEFT JOIN to ensure suppliers with flawless records (zero downtime) remain in the final financial report.

Data Cleansing: Applied COALESCE() to handle NULL values generated by non-delayed orders, converting them to 0 for accurate financial summation.

Conditional Logic: Used CASE WHEN extensively for boolean flagging (On-Time / In-Full) and generating dynamic text labels for Risk Categorization.

 Database Schema
The analysis is built on a relational structure consisting of three core tables:

suppliers: Contains vendor master data, origin country, and material categories (e.g., Electronics, Mechanics).

purchase_orders: Tracks order dates, expected vs. actual delivery dates, and ordered vs. received quantities.

production_logs: Logs factory incidents, downtime hours, and the hourly cost of the halted assembly line, directly linked to specific purchase orders.

 Key Findings & Business Insights
(Based on the sample dataset analyzed)

Reliability Champion: EuroPlast achieved a 100% OTIF rate with zero days of delay, resulting in €0 downtime cost. Classified as Low Risk - Reliable Supplier.

Moderate Risk: TechVantage GmbH delivered only 50% of orders successfully. Their 3-day average delay caused an assembly line halt, resulting in €22,500 in financial loss. Classified as High Risk - Needs Review.

Critical Bottleneck: Global Steel Parts failed to meet the OTIF criteria for all orders (0% OTIF), averaging a 5-day delay. This caused severe machining process halts, costing the company €70,000. Classified as Critical Risk - Immediate Action.

 Conclusion
By linking procurement metrics (OTIF) directly to manufacturing execution metrics (Downtime Cost), this SQL pipeline provides supply chain managers with a transparent, monetary-based risk assessment. This allows the business to renegotiate contracts with high-risk vendors and secure secondary suppliers for critical components before production lines are compromised.
